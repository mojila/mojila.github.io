When to add: ex-dividend dates

The ex-date is the first session a share trades without its next distribution. The price usually opens lower by roughly the payout — which is why it can look like a dip. Dates below are the December 2026 cycle; amounts are estimates until declared.

Holding Next ex-date Est. drop / share Cash paid on Recurring pattern DCA window

Buy before the ex-date only if you specifically want the distribution. Buy on or after it if you are accumulating: the payout is already out of the price, so you avoid receiving — and being taxed at source on — a distribution you effectively paid for. For JPIN the December payout is unusually large, so this timing choice matters more than for the others. No ex-date dip is free money; it is the distribution leaving the price.

How the prediction is built. BBRI pays twice a year: an interim declared in December and paid mid-January, then a final declared after the AGM and paid around May. The last cycle was Rp137 + Rp209 = Rp346 per share, which is also the trailing-12-month figure Pluang reports as an 11.16% yield. The forecast simply carries that forward — interim near 40% of the annual total, final near 60% — and brackets it by Rp46 either side. That is a distribution assumption, not a company forecast.

Three things this does not know. (1) Cost basis was not supplied, so yield-on-cost cannot be shown — only yield on today's price. (2) Tax: 10% final withholding is assumed, which is the rate for an Indonesian resident individual; a different tax status changes every net figure. (3) The EPS series Pluang returns for BBRI is internally inconsistent (quarterly figures near Rp400 against a P/E of 8.2 at Rp3,100, which implies annual EPS near Rp378), so no payout-ratio maths was used to derive the forecast — it rests on declared dividends only. Verify the numbers before acting.

What counts as “income”

A distribution is only cash if the shares were held through the ex-dividend date. Your December figure uses each fund's typical payment month, not its declaration date, so a quarterly fund with a December ex-date but a January pay date lands in January here.

Covered-call funds like JEPQ can pay out of option premium and return of capital, so their headline yield is not equivalent to interest.

Why the DCA split looks like this

VOO over SPY for new money: same index, lower expense ratio, fractional shares available. JPIN is the only developed ex-US exposure and is the most oversold position on Pluang's daily oscillators (RSI-12 ≈ 25). JEPQ is the income engine.

No new GLD or SLV. Metals are already ~20% of invested capital; SLV is down a third and still the worst momentum in the book.

The 3.38% hurdle

Your USD cash yields ~3.38% p.a. Every dollar moved into equities has to beat that after fees and tax. This plan's blended distribution yield is shown above — income alone may clear the hurdle, but price risk is the reason to keep contributions modest and regular.

Ticket friction is real: a $500 ticket at ~0.3% all-in costs about $1.50, or roughly two weeks of that money's cash interest.
